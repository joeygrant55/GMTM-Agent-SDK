# Market Research — US Youth Sports Recruiting (compiled July 2026)

Research basis: multi-angle web research (market sizing, competitor pricing/reputation,
2024–26 structural shifts, AI entrants, acquisition channels/compliance), July 2026.
Sources linked inline. Figures marked *(inference)* are ours, not reported.

## The market in five numbers

1. **$40B+/yr** US parent spend on youth sports. Average family spent **$1,016 on one
   child's primary sport in 2024, up 46% since 2019**; $150K+ households spend ~$2,068.
   ([Project Play/Aspen](https://projectplay.org/news/2025/2/24/project-play-survey-family-spending-on-youth-sports-rises-46-over-five-years),
   [Empower](https://www.empower.com/the-currency/play/game-on-the-40-billion-play-in-youth-sports-news))
2. **~8M HS athletes → ~7% NCAA → <2% D1 → ~2% athletic scholarship.**
   ([NCAA probability research](https://www.ncaa.org/sports/2015/3/2/estimated-probability-of-competing-in-college-athletics.aspx),
   [NCAA recruiting fact sheet](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/NCAA_RecruitingFactSheet.pdf))
   Yet **83% of families** want a roster spot only ~7% will get (IMG survey via
   [Aspen parent survey 2025](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/National-Youth-Sports-Parent-Survey-FINAL.pdf)).
3. **NCSA (IMG Academy): 175K premium members, 4.5M free profiles, 30,800 commitments in 2024**
   at $1,000–$3,000+ packages ([IMG](https://www.imgacademy.com/news/img-academys-ncsa-college-recruiting-product-sets-record))
   → implies a business in the hundreds of millions of dollars *(inference)*.
4. **Transfer portal displacement:** 14,000+ D1 transfers in 2025 vs 9,760 in 2023; coaches
   fill spots from the portal before evaluating HS seniors.
   ([SI](https://www.si.com/high-school/recruiting/how-the-transfer-portal-is-quietly-crushing-high-school-recruiting-01kb94hf4k9w),
   [2aDays](https://www.2adays.com/blog/the-new-recruiting-battleground-high-school-vs-transfer-portal/))
5. **House settlement (June 2025):** scholarship limits replaced by hard roster caps
   (football 105 vs ~180-man rosters); est. 5,000–13,000 roster spots eliminated; walk-ons
   effectively ending. ([NCAA](https://www.ncaa.org/news/2025/6/23/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits.aspx),
   [2aDays](https://www.2adays.com/blog/roster-caps-revenue-sharing-why-walk-ons-may-disappear-2026/))

## Eight decision-relevant facts

1. **Price squeeze from both ends.** Free-and-verified below (Scorability, ChatGPT), $1K–3K
   human services above, $9.99 AI beside. A $29/mo product must visibly *do work*, not answer questions.
2. **The parent pays; the athlete uses.** Every incumbent sells to parents. SPARQ currently
   has no parent-facing surface.
3. **NCSA's trust deficit is a positioning gift** — transparent pricing + cancel-anytime +
   honest odds is the counter-message.
4. **Post-House, honest multi-division advising is on-trend.** D1 supply is shrinking;
   families need realistic D2/D3/NAIA/JUCO maps. The Honest Assessment artifact fits the moment.
5. **Distribution is B2B2C or death.** Clubs, high schools, tournaments — not D2C ads.
6. **Verified data is the emerging moat** (Scorability bought a camp company for it). Ours:
   75K profiles, 131K metrics, 7,832 scholarship outcomes, MaxPreps ingestion — plus
   coach open/reply data from outreach sent through SPARQ (compounding, unique).
7. **Demand is seasonal** (June 15 / Sept 1 contact dates, signing periods) → monthly subs
   churn between seasons; annual/season packaging matters.
8. **Compliance is a launch constraint**: NCAA recruiting-service bylaws
   ([NCSA's posture](https://www.ncsasports.org/how-ncsa-works/ncaa-certification-compliance-scouting-services)),
   COPPA / minors' data ([overview](https://www.activecomply.com/compliance-resources/coppa-compliance-essential-guidelines-for-marketers-targeting-children)).

## Strategic read (July 2026)

The *market* is large, growing, and emotionally charged. The *specific lane* of a paid D2C
consumer subscription is structurally hard: squeezed pricing, a well-funded free competitor,
seasonal churn, and channel economics that favor club distribution.

**Strongest consumer-app shape given SPARQ's assets:** "the honest AI agent that works your
recruiting for you" — free honest baseline (Quick Scan vs 75K real athletes) as the growth
engine; the working weekly agent loop (Scout/Drafter/Analyst → approve → send → track replies);
a **Sunday parent brief** so the payer sees the work; the coach-responsiveness dataset as the
compounding moat. Pricing: free tier with real value; Pro $29/mo or $199/yr framed against
NCSA's $3,000. Channel: free club-coach surface (gift Quick Scans to rosters) → B2B2C.

**Primary risk to watch:** Scorability adding an advisory layer on top of free — the window
for the "works for the family, not for colleges" position is open now.

**Kill list:** video pipeline (Hudl owns it), 14 unbuilt agents, legacy /athlete workspace,
one-time report SKUs (conflicts with subscription).

## Caveats

- Full-page source verification was limited in the research environment; figures rely on the
  cited publications' own reporting. Competitor claims (NCSA's "25% of freshman roster spots,"
  Scorability's user counts) are self-reported and should be discounted accordingly.
